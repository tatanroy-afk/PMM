# ICP Profile: SAP Ariba (Mid-Market Push, Tiered)

> **Built using:** `/icp-definition` from the PMM skillset
> **Scope:** SAP Ariba (one product within the SAP Finance & Spend portfolio)
> **Business objective served:** Push Ariba down-market to the $500M-$2B revenue band while protecting deal quality.
> **Data caveat:** Layer 4 (Language) sourced from public category-level signal (Spend Matters, Hackett, G2 reviews, Procurement Foundry, ProcureCon, analyst reports) — *not* SAP customer interviews. **Sharpening required**: 3-5 Tier 1 customer interviews + Gong mining within 30 days.

---

## Product Context

**Product category:**
> Enterprise source-to-pay suite (sourcing, contracts, procurement, supplier management, invoicing) for indirect + direct spend, sold to procurement leaders at companies running SAP S/4HANA or actively migrating to it. Sold into both SAP and non-SAP ERP accounts, with structural advantage on SAP environments.

**Business objective (next 6-12 months):**
> Push down-market to the $500M-$2B revenue band while reducing the bad-fit lead bleed currently contaminating mid-market pipeline.

**Competitive alternatives in scope:** Coupa, Ivalua, GEP Smart, "do nothing / build on S/4HANA MM."

**Strategic constraint surfaced during ICP build:**
> SAP Ariba has no deep bench of $500M-$2B reference customers. NatureSweet (2018) is the only credibly documented mid-market case study. Coupa is winning even inside SAP-ERP customers (Sanofi, with 7 SAP instances, chose Coupa). Therefore: **the mid-market push must be narrowed to a defensible sub-segment, and the anti-ICP must do surgical filtering work.**

---

## TIERED ICP STRUCTURE

This is a **tiered ICP**, not a single profile:

- **Tier 1 ICP** — $1B-$2B revenue. Clean pass on firmographics; full sales motion.
- **Tier 2 ICP** — $500M-$1B revenue. Must pass 4 hard behavioral qualifiers (Layer 2) before entering pipeline.

Tier 1 responds to **aspiration** (strategic transformation, COO career path). Tier 2 responds to **survival** (visible quick wins, defend the function). Messaging must segment accordingly.

## LAYER 2 — BEHAVIORAL

### Buying Triggers

| Trigger | Tier | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **S/4HANA migration in motion** (RISE signed; ECC EOL 2027 forcing the move) | T1 + T2 | Highest-signal trigger. Coupa loses on integration cost in this window. |
| **Acquisition-driven systems fragmentation** | T1 | 3+ acquisitions in 24 months → spend visibility broken → CFO demands consolidation. |

| **Procurement leadership change** | T1 + T2 | New CPO/VP Procurement hired in last 12 months with a "modernize" mandate. |
| **Audit finding or compliance event** | T1 | SOX, FCPA, LkSG, CSDDD, ESG supplier-DD finding → board demands traceability. |
| **Coupa contract renewal** | T1 + T2 | 3 years into Coupa, price climbing 15% YoY, S/4HANA migration as the wedge to switch. |

### Current Alternatives in This Segment
- SAP MM native + Excel sourcing (internal cannibalization risk)
- Coupa (the displacement target if already modernized)
- Oracle Procurement Cloud (PeopleSoft → S/4HANA migrations sometimes defended by Oracle Fusion)
- Fragmented post-acquisition stack (Ivalua + Jaggaer + Excel across BUs)
- JAGGAER (direct-spend manufacturing, especially specialty chemicals)

### Evaluation Behavior
- **Consultant-led at this segment** — Deloitte, Accenture, EY, Capgemini, KPMG run the RFP. The SI partner is the kingmaker.
- 3-4 vendors evaluated in parallel
- POC/sandbox rare; analyst reports + consultant recommendations are the trust source
- Gartner Magic Quadrant + Forrester Wave + Peer Insights = required reading
- ProcureCon and SAPPHIRE attendance both signal active eval

### Decision Process
- **Recommender:** CPO / VP Procurement
- **Approver:** CFO (T1) or CFO + COO jointly (T2)
- **Integrator / Veto:** CIO or VP Enterprise Applications (de facto veto via integration complexity)
- **Kingmaker:** External SI partner practice lead
- **Block source:** Internal SAP MM team that built custom workflows on ECC

### Timeline
- **Tier 1:** 6-12 months trigger → contract (bound to S/4HANA program timeline)
- **Tier 2:** 12-18 months (function-build + tool-eval concurrent; this is the Tier 2 trap)

### Tier 2 Hard Behavioral Qualifiers
*SDR must confirm on call #1. If any fails, account does not enter pipeline.*

1. ☐ S/4HANA program board-approved or active (not "exploring")
2. ☐ Named CPO/VP Procurement employed today (not "we're hiring one")
3. ☐ 500+ active suppliers in indirect spend
4. ☐ Capital budget for procurement transformation signed off for current FY ($2-5M minimum)

### Content Consumption / Where They Learn
- Gartner / Forrester reports (filtered through their consultant)
- ProcureCon, ISM World, SAPPHIRE, SIG Symposium
- Spend Matters newsletter, MyPurchasingCenter, Procurement Magazine
- Art of Procurement podcast (Philip Ideson)

## LAYER 3 — PSYCHOGRAPHIC

### Beliefs
- "Procurement is a strategic function, not an admin one."
- "Supplier risk = enterprise risk." (Post-COVID, post-Ukraine, post-Red Sea conviction.)
- "ERP migration is the once-a-decade chance to fix procurement properly."
- "Savings is table stakes; value creation is what makes us strategic."

### Frustrations
- CEO talks about supply chain in every board meeting, but the function reports to CFO who only asks about savings.
- Team is 30-50% understaffed vs. peers per Hackett benchmarks — *they know the number*.
- Category managers stuck doing tactical RFPs instead of strategic category work.
- **The rage point:** The S/4HANA program is run by IT and Finance; procurement is "consulted" but not deciding the design.
- Suppliers complain about onboarding, but no budget for supplier experience tools.

### Identity
- **See themselves as:** "The only person in this company who connects supplier risk, cost, and ESG into one strategic view."
- **Want to be seen as:** Strategic operator. Peer to CFO/COO. Future COO candidate.
- **Refuse to be seen as:** "Buying department." "Sourcing guys." Back-office.

### Fears (the 11pm-Sunday fears)
- S/4HANA goes live with procurement bolted on → 5 years of cleanup, career-defining failure.
- Supplier event (recall, sanctions, fraud, ESG scandal) hits Bloomberg, procurement gets named.
- Finance absorbs procurement under "SG&A optimization" in the next cost cycle.
- CIO positions S/4HANA as an IT program and freezes procurement out of design decisions.

### Aspirations
- COO seat in 5 years (or CFO at a smaller company; or board director).
- Speak at ProcureCon or ISM World keynote within 3 years.
- Name-dropped in Hackett, Spend Matters, or Deloitte thought leadership.
- Run procurement as a profit center, not a cost function.

### Tribal Markers
- Member: Procurement Foundry, Sourcing Industry Group (SIG)
- Reads: Spend Matters, MyPurchasingCenter, Art of Procurement
- Attends: ISM World, ProcureCon, SAPPHIRE
- Quotes: Christopher Sawchuk (Hackett), Philip Ideson (AoP), Pierre Mitchell (Spend Matters), Kelly Barner
- Posts about: intake-to-procure, should-cost, scope 3, tail spend, spend under management, supplier diversity, autonomous sourcing
- Vocabulary signal: "intake" not "requisition"; "category strategy" not "sourcing plan"; "spend under management" not "addressable spend"; "should-cost" not "target price"

### Tier 2 Psychographic Variation
- First-in-seat (promoted 12-24 months ago or company's first CPO hire)
- Political capital fragile
- Caught between building the function and delivering savings simultaneously
- Less tribal — not yet in Procurement Foundry, no LinkedIn following
- Deeper fear: "I'll be replaced in 18 months if this doesn't show ROI fast"

## ANTI-ICP

### Deal Breakers (5 Dimensions)

| Dimension | Disqualifies |
|---|---|
| **Resources** | <$500M revenue; PE-owned <24-month hold; declining revenue in current FY; capex not approved. |
| **Program** | Not in or planning S/4HANA migration within 24 months. |
| **Access** | CFO-led eval without CPO; 8+ steering-committee stakeholders without executive sponsor. |
| **Team** | No dedicated procurement function; reports to ops 3 levels down; AP team <3; no category mgmt. |
| **Stage** | First procurement hire <12 months ago AND no budget; turnaround; just-completed major M&A still integrating. |

### Resource-Drain Indicators
- Sourcing-module-only intent
- Custom non-SAP ERP integration requirement
- Champion hired <12 months ago without 3-year mandate
- POC/sandbox extension requests beyond 60 days
- Supplier base <500 active suppliers
- Procurement steering committee with 8+ stakeholders, no decision deadline
- Asking for case studies of <$1B Ariba customers

### Tiered Exclusions

| Tier | Definition | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Hard no** | Multiple deal-breakers; will never succeed | Disqualify on call #1; don't book discovery |
| **Not now** | Right firmographics, missing one prerequisite | Nurture; re-engagement trigger (RISE signed / CPO 18-month tenure) |
| **Looks like ICP but isn't** ⚠️ | Matches firmographics, fails behavioral/psychographic | **Train SDRs to spot in call #1.** This is the pipeline contamination tier. |

### Red Flag Checklist (SDR Call #1)

```
ARIBA ANTI-ICP RED FLAGS

[ ] Revenue < $500M (instant disqualify)
[ ] Industry: SaaS/tech, financial services, pure services, declining retail
[ ] Not on SAP ERP and no S/4HANA migration planned in 24 months
[ ] Non-SAP ERP with explicit "ERP-agnostic" requirement
[ ] No named CPO or VP Procurement employed
```

## STAKEHOLDER SUMMARY (copy-paste ready)

> Our ICP is the **CPO or VP Procurement at a $500M-$2B revenue process-heavy company** (CPG, pharma, specialty chemicals, industrial manufacturing) in North America, Western Europe, or APAC tier-1, **with a dedicated procurement function and an S/4HANA migration in motion within 24 months**.
>
> They buy when the **S/4HANA program creates a once-a-decade window to fix procurement properly** — combined with CFO pressure, M&A-driven systems fragmentation, a new procurement leader's mandate, or a compliance/audit trigger.
>
> They choose us over **Coupa, Ivalua, GEP, and "build on S/4HANA MM"** because the S/4HANA integration window favors a native SAP stack — *but only when they have the procurement-ops capacity to deploy*.
>
> We are explicitly **NOT for**:
> - Sub-$500M companies
> - Tech, financial services, pure services, declining retail
> - Non-SAP ERP accounts without an S/4HANA migration plan
> - Companies without a named CPO or signed procurement transformation budget

>
> By sharpening to this tiered ICP, we expect to **reduce mid-market bad-fit leads by ~40% within two quarters** while maintaining logo-count growth in Tier 1 ($1B-$2B) accounts where Ariba has structural advantage.
